Every page, every role, walked and scored.

A Playwright pass over both Sero apps as a logged-out visitor, a member, a manager and an internal admin. Every route loaded, every call to action listed, the safe ones clicked, and every screen measured for contrast, target size, copy rules and motion. No paid runs, nothing written to live.

The verdict

Scorecard

Scored on what the walk actually showed, not on intent. Sero is in good shape. The writing is the best thing in it, the shell is coherent, nothing crashed, and every role wall held. Two defects are worth stopping for.

6.5/10
UX

Flows are short and the structure is mostly right. Held back by the run-detail reload dead end, the Team and Members ambiguity, and how thin the member side is.

6/10
UI

Genuinely handsome and consistent. One shell, one card style, one grid. Marked down for the primary-button contrast and the sidebar stopping mid page.

7/10
Writing

The screen copy is the strongest thing in the product: plain, warm, no jargon. Marked down because the em-dash rule is broken in 35 of 41 briefings, and two date formats sit in one list.

4.5/10
Motion

Effectively absent. Hover states only, no transitions between states, no visible loading. Nothing about the interface moves when the data does.

7/10
Coherence

It hangs together and it makes sense. The gaps are at the seams: two people lists, an admin shell that ejects managers into a different app, one screen that is only a card.

The two to fix before anything else The primary button fails colour contrast on every screen in the product (2.5:1 against a 4.5:1 bar), and opening a past 1:1 then refreshing loses the page. Both are small changes. Both are felt by every manager, every day.

criticalUXManager (both apps), Admin

F1 Open a past 1:1, press refresh, lose the page

Click a person in Past 1:1s and the address bar becomes /runs/<id>. Reload that exact address and Sero rewrites it back to /runs and shows an empty card: “NO 1:1 SELECTED. Pick one from your 1:1s list.” Browser Back is dead too. The address comes back, the empty card stays.

Why it matters. It is the app’s own URL. A manager who refreshes, bookmarks a 1:1, or opens the link on another device hits a dead end, and nothing on screen says the 1:1 still exists.

Fix. Boot needs to honour /runs/:id for a manager the way it already honours /team/:person. The customer router.js parses the id correctly; boot never acts on it outside the member lane.

criticalUIAll

F2 Every primary button fails colour contrast

The action blue #5aa9e6 with white text measures 2.54:1 against a 4.5:1 bar. It is on Log in, Start 1:1, Add person, Invite people, Continue, Add request and Save note. That is the entire action layer, both apps, all three roles.

Why it matters. The most important control on every screen is the hardest thing on it to read. On a dimmed laptop or in daylight the label sinks into the fill, and it reads as a disabled button.

Fix. The palette already holds the answer. --sero-primary-800 (#1b5d91) gives 6.97:1. Keep #5aa9e6 for accents, active nav and links, and swap only the solid-button fill. One token, whole app.

highUIAdmin (internal screens)

F3 The sidebar stops halfway down every long page

The app shell is sized to the window, not the document. Measured: the shell renders 900px tall on Library (2,641px of page), User management (2,115px), All runs (11,680px) and Design system (12,040px). Below 900px the navy nav simply stops and a bare white column runs to the bottom.

Why it matters. Scroll down the Library and the app looks broken. The shell falls away and the content floats on white. It happens on every long internal screen.

Fix. Make the nav position: sticky at height: 100vh inside a full-height flex shell, or paint the shell background on the layout container so the column reads as deliberate.

highUIManager, Admin

F4 Page-header buttons collide with the account chip

Measured at 1440×900: the account chip occupies x 1108 to 1428, y 12 to 52. On Past 1:1s the Start 1:1 button sits at x 1255 to 1356, y 48 to 96, and on Library Archived (7) sits at x 1224 to 1356, y 48 to 96. Both sit directly beneath the chip and overlap it. Pages that carry the small eyebrow line (“Work”, “Account”) push the button clear. Pages without one do not.

Why it matters. Two of the most-used buttons in the product tuck under the account chip and read as clipped. The header stops looking designed.

Fix. Have the eyebrow slot reserve its height when empty, and give the header a min-height so the action always clears the chip.

highUXManager

F5 “Team” and “Members” sit side by side and neither name explains itself

Team is the people you run 1:1s with. Members is the accounts that can log in. They are adjacent in the nav, both are lists of people with avatars, and the same human can appear in both.

Why it matters. This is the one place a new manager has to hold two different meanings of “person” at once. Adding someone to the wrong list gets them nothing, with no error to explain it.

Fix. Name them by intent: “My people” and “Workspace access”. Or fold access into Team as a column and drop the second screen entirely.

highUXManager

F16 A manager can demote an admin

On Members, the row menu on an admin row offers Make manager and Switch off access to a plain manager. Reading the code behind it: members.service.setRole accepts any target inside the caller’s org and only guards against removing the last active lead. There is no check that the person doing it outranks the person it is done to. Deactivation is better protected: a superadmin email cannot be switched off.

Why it matters. Anyone you invite as a manager can quietly change the role of the account that runs the workspace. It is a trust question rather than a bug: nothing here is malicious today, but the door is open.

Fix. Add a rank check to setRole: a manager may only change a member or another manager, never an admin. Then hide the menu on admin rows for non-admins.

Read from the code path and the rendered menu. The mutation itself was not fired.

highWritingEvery manager who reads a briefing

F7 The em-dash rule is broken where it matters most: inside the briefings

npm run lint:copy passes, and it is not wrong. It scans admin/src and frontend/src, 248 files, and those are clean. Everything downstream of that is unguarded. 35 of the 41 finished briefings in the dev org contain em dashes. The Meeting arcs screen renders 7. The run review page renders 3. There are 144 quoted em-dash strings in backend/engine outside the tests, including a fixed one a manager reads: “This didn’t come up in the conversation — not enough signal to read.” (reviewer.ts:362). And answer-suggester.ts:56 actively tells the model: Use “→” for cause/result and “—” for a trailing detail when it fits.

Why it matters. The rule exists because these are the words a manager reads before a real conversation. Right now the engine is being taught to break it, so the more Sero writes, the more the rule is broken.

Fix. Three steps, in order. Delete the em-dash instruction in answer-suggester.ts:56. Add backend/engine and content/data to SCAN_DIRS in scripts/lint-copy.js and clear what it finds. Then add a guard on generated prose at the point it is saved, because a source scan can never catch what the model writes at runtime.

mediumUIAdmin

F6 Six brand marks are broken on the Design system page

The Brandmark row asks for /sero-flowbite/brand/*.svg and gets six 404s. The files exist. They sit behind the admin app’s /admin base, so the root-absolute path misses them.

Why it matters. The page that is meant to be the source of truth for the brand renders the brand as missing images.

Fix. Prefix the paths with import.meta.env.BASE_URL, the way google-g.svg already does.

mediumUXAdmin

F8 The error log shows raw SQL to a human

103 issues, 10,047px of page, no paging, and the “What went wrong” column prints whole insert statements: Failed query: insert into "people" ("id", "org_id", …) values (default, $1, $2…. That is 3.3 million characters of text on one screen.

Why it matters. It is the screen you open when something is on fire, and it is the least readable screen in the product.

Fix. Show the first clause only, keep the full statement in the row’s detail view, and page at 50.

mediumUIAll

F17 The build stamp is a click-blocking overlay, and it ships to live

The build chip is a position: fixed button, 243×27px, bottom right, z-index: 9999, pointer-events: auto, on every screen of both apps. document.elementFromPoint at its centre returns the chip, so nothing under it can be clicked. Its own source comment calls it “an always-on chip”, not a dev-only one. During the automated pass it swallowed the click on roughly 30 controls.

Why it matters. It sits exactly where a table’s last row, a pagination control or a footer action lands. It is invisible as a problem until someone cannot click something and does not know why.

Fix. pointer-events: none on the chip, with pointer-events: auto only on the SHA span if click-to-copy matters. Or hide it behind a keystroke.

mediumUXMember, Manager

F18 “Add request” with an empty box does nothing and says nothing

On the member’s Home, clicking Add request with the text box empty produces no change at all: no error, no hint, no shake, and the button is not disabled. Confirmed by comparing the full page text before and after the click. On the intake screen the same situation is handled properly: Continue with nothing chosen says “Pick someone, or add a name to continue.”

Why it matters. It is the member’s only interactive control in the entire product. Pressing it and getting silence teaches them the feature is broken.

Fix. Reuse the intake pattern: a one-line message under the field. The copy already exists in that voice.

mediumUIAll

F9 Click targets under the comfortable minimum

Row overflow menus are 34×26px, the nav collapse is 28×28px, star ratings are 25×24px, the account chip is 28×22px, filter checkboxes are 18×18px. The floor is 44×44 for touch and 32×32 at the very least for a mouse.

Why it matters. These are the controls a manager reaches for most: rename a person, open a run, rate a prep.

Fix. Grow the hit area without changing the look: a ::before inset of -8px, or a 44px min tap box on the button.

mediumUXMember

F10 The member’s side of Sero is one card deep

A member can reach exactly one screen. Every other address, including /runs/<their own 1:1>, bounces to /home. Home shows a single latest 1:1 (date and type only), a Requests box, and an empty Goals card. The nav item says “Your 1:1s”; the page says “Home” and lists one.

Why it matters. Half the value story is that the member sees something too. Today that is a date, a label and a text box.

Fix. Own it or open it. Either match the nav label to the page and say plainly why only the date is shown, or give the list its own screen. The no-inference ruling limits what is shown, not how well it is framed.

lowWritingManager, Admin

F11 Two date formats in the same list

Home and Past 1:1s mix relative and absolute in adjacent rows: “2d ago”, “5d ago”, then “Fri 17 Jul 2026”. User management goes further with “last active no runs yet · 0 runs”.

Why it matters. You cannot compare two rows without doing arithmetic, and “last active no runs yet” is not a sentence.

Fix. Relative under seven days, absolute after, applied to the whole column rather than per row. And write the empty state as “never active”.

lowMotionAll

F12 Nothing moves

Across 256 page loads, 3 pages carried a running CSS animation, and the average page had 33 elements with a transition, almost all of them hover colour changes. There is no page transition, no list stagger, no animated state change, and no skeleton in the DOM on a settled page.

Why it matters. The app feels correct but inert. Motion is usually where a product says “someone thought about this”, and here it says nothing at all.

Fix. Three cheap wins: a 120ms cross-fade on stage change, a 40ms-per-row stagger on list mount, and a skeleton that is actually visible on first fetch.

lowUXManager, Member

F13 Half a screen of empty space on most pages

Measured at 1440×900: the manager’s Home ends at 460px of 900, the member’s Home at 589px, Past 1:1s at 614px, Team at 654px, Members at 686px. On the intake screen the content column runs 556 to 1132px, leaving a 308px dead gutter on the right and an off-centre look.

Why it matters. The product looks like it holds less than it does. There is room for the next 1:1 due, the person’s recent themes, or the prep left half finished.

Fix. The guest lane already gets this right: it drops the sidebar for the flow and centres the column. Do the same for a signed-in manager. Then give the list pages a right rail: next up, unfinished prep, recently added.

lowUILogged out

F14 The logged-out screens are not in the brand typeface

Every in-app screen sets its headings in Bricolage Grotesque. The front door, Log in, Create account and the password screens set theirs in Inter. That is 74 of 256 page loads.

Why it matters. The three screens a new manager meets first are the three that do not look like Sero.

Fix. Apply the display face in auth.css, or decide it is deliberate and write that down in DESIGN.md.

lowUIAll

F15 Search boxes have no name for a screen reader

The list toolbar’s search input carries a placeholder and nothing else. No label, no aria-label. It appears on 10 screens including Team, Members, Library and User management.

Why it matters. A screen-reader user hears “edit text” and has to guess, and the placeholder disappears the moment they type.

Fix. One aria-label in list-toolbar.ts fixes all ten.

Role by role

What each person can actually reach

The role walls hold. A member cannot reach a manager screen, a manager cannot reach the internal console, and a logged-out visitor gets the guest run lane and nothing else. Every attempt below was a real deep link, not a nav click.

One thing to decide, not fix A manager who opens any internal address is ejected out of the admin app entirely and lands on the customer app’s home. It is safe and it works, but it is a hard bounce with no message. A short “that is an internal screen” line would cost nothing.

Every button, clicked

963 controls were clicked and the resulting state recorded. Destructive and session-ending controls (log out, delete, invite, send, anything that starts a paid run) were listed but deliberately not fired. Of those clicked, 95 navigated, 212 changed the page in place and 47 opened a panel or menu.

Read this section as a shortlist, not a bug list The harness calls a click “no visible change” when the page text moves by fewer than 25 characters, which is a blunt test. Spot-checking proved it: the Library row menu registers as no change because its menu adds only 20 characters, yet it opens perfectly. The one entry below that was chased down and confirmed is Add request on the member Home, which is now F18. The 100 “could not click” rows are almost all the build stamp intercepting the pointer (F17) or an element re-rendering between listing and clicking, not app faults.
