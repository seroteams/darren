01 · Focus points

A fixed menu, a smart waiter

The engine cannot invent a focus point. It picks 2 to 5 from a closed menu of 21, then rewrites the labels around your note. If your note is empty, the pick follows a recipe keyed to seniority and meeting type.

03 · Question bank

The richest picture, frozen too early

This is the engine at its most connected: one AI call that reads nearly everything you gave it and writes 8 to 12 tailored questions. The catch: it happens once, before the first answer exists, and is never refreshed. Everything the meeting later "adapts" is drawn from this pre-meeting pool.

04 · The live meeting

The planner sees plenty. It can change little.

Each turn, the planner AI reads the whole transcript, your last answer and the running scores, and may reword or reorder what is left. But the pool was written before turn 1, several slots are pinned, and a skipped question skips the AI entirely: the next turn comes back byte-identical.

05 · The final brief

Everything arrives, one thing is missing

After the meeting the wiring is at its best: transcript, your mid-meeting notes, scores, answer quality, agenda, promises all land in the evaluation. The one absentee is the prep brief itself, so Sero can never tell you whether the meeting did what you set out to do.

07 · Where I would start

Three fixes that turn script into picture

A

Let the meeting reshape the plan. After the first real answers, refresh the remaining questions from what was actually said, instead of serving a pool written before turn 1. This is the single biggest "it gets me" unlock.

⭐ biggest effect
B

Give the planner your notes. Wire mid-meeting notes and the intake note into the live planner. Small change, immediately visible connective feel.

smallest lift
C

Make the signals matter. Thin answers, declines and falling scores should change pacing: chase what is dropping, stop drilling what is closed, close the loop in the final brief against the prep brief.
