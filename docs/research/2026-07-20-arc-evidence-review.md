# Sero 1:1 Arc Design: Evidence Review and Revision Recommendations

## TL;DR
- **Four of the five arcs are broadly defensible; the "Performance & feedback" arc's "direct, no cushioning" tone and its "Self-read → Evidence" opening are the two places Sero diverges from the evidence and should change first.** The remaining arcs align well with coaching, engagement, onboarding and wellbeing research once minor phase merges are made.
- **6–9 questions is realistic and, if anything, on the high side.** With a direct report doing 70–80% of the talking, a 30-minute 1:1 supports roughly 4–6 substantive manager questions; the 8- and 9-question arcs assume a 45–60 minute slot and should be gated to that length or trimmed.
- **The repeated 1-2-2-1 shape is defensible, not a defect** — the conversation *types* are genuinely differentiated by phase content and tone, which is what the evidence says matters, not by arithmetic shape. The risk is not the shape but tone-bleed between relational and evaluative meetings, which is exactly what deterministic gates should police.

## Key Findings

**1. 1:1s work, but most are executed poorly, and the direct report should own the floor.** Steven Rogelberg's research (Glad We Met, OUP 2024) reports that "nearly 50 percent of employees found their 1:1s lacked substance and were suboptimal, even when their managers rated them as positive" — with roughly 200 million 1:1s occurring globally per day at an estimated $1.25 billion daily cost. His core prescription: the meeting should be "dominated by topics of importance to the direct report rather than issues that are top of mind for the manager" (HBR, 2022). Gallup's *State of the American Manager* (2015; variance-decomposition by Beck & Harter across 2.7M employees in ~100,000 teams) found "managers account for at least 70% of the variance in employee engagement scores across business units," and that "employees who meet regularly with their managers are nearly three times more likely to be engaged at work" (only ~15% of employees without regular check-ins are engaged). This validates Sero's manager-prep-first, employee-led design premise.

**2. Feedback that is task-directed beats feedback that is person-directed — this is the single strongest quantitative finding relevant to Sero.** Kluger & DeNisi's meta-analysis (Psychological Bulletin, 119(2):254–284, 1996; 607 effect sizes, 23,663 observations) found feedback interventions raised performance on average (weighted mean d = 0.41) but that "over 1/3 of the FIs decreased performance" (over 38% of effects negative, p.258). The decisive mechanism: "FI effectiveness decreases as attention moves up the hierarchy closer to the self and away from the task." **This directly challenges Sero's "direct, no cushioning" tone label for the Performance arc.** The evidence does not say "cushion"; it says "keep attention on the task, not the person." "No cushioning" is defensible if it means "no vague sugar-coating" (consistent with Radical Candor's Ruinous Empathy trap — Kim Scott's warning against sugar-coated, unclear criticism), but dangerous if the runner allows person-coded language.

**3. Self-assessment first is supported for a developmental/relational purpose but not for rating accuracy.** The foundational GE studies (Meyer, Kay & French, "Split Roles in Performance Appraisal," HBR 1965) found manager criticism "generates defensiveness ... which leads to poorer performance," while "participation by the subordinate ... produces performance improvement." Bassett & Meyer (Personnel Psychology, 1968) found self-review-based appraisal decreased defensiveness and produced more constructive interviews. Cawley, Keeping & Levy's meta-analysis (Journal of Applied Psychology, 83(4):615–633, 1998; 27 studies, 32 samples) found "the overall relationship (ρ) between participation and employee reactions, corrected for unreliability, was .61," with "value-expressive participation (i.e., participation for the sake of having one's voice heard)" most strongly related to satisfaction. **However**, Mabe & West's meta-analysis (JAP, 1982; 55 studies) found self-evaluations correlate with objective performance at only r = 0.29 — self-ratings are inflated and weakly valid. Implication for Sero: opening the Performance arc with "Self-read(1)" is well-supported *as a defensiveness-reducer and voice mechanism*, provided the manager's evidence still follows and the self-read is not treated as the rating.

**4. Solution-focused questions outperform problem-focused questions.** Grant & Gerrard (2019) and Grant & O'Connor (2010) found solution-focused coaching questions produced larger gains in positive affect, self-efficacy and goal orientation and larger reductions in negative affect than problem-focused questions (e.g., ~11% reduction in negative affect, ~10% increase in positive affect in the SF condition; both statistically significant). This supports Sero's existing "what/how over why" phrasing rule and the "Momentum" and "Lift" phases of the check-in.

**5. GROW is a validated coaching spine with a moderate effect.** *Implementing the GROW Coaching Model in Developing Competencies of Educators: A Meta-Analysis* (International Journal of Social Learning, Vol. 6(2), 2026) analysed "22 empirical studies... random-effects model... pooled effect size of 0.43, indicating a moderate level of effectiveness with no evidence of publication bias." GROW (Goal → Reality → Options → Will) maps cleanly onto Sero's Growth arc (Aspiration≈Goal, Gap≈Reality, Investment≈Options, Commitment≈Will), which is the arc that most closely tracks an established framework.

**6. SBI/COIN feedback frameworks converge on observation-before-interpretation.** The Center for Creative Leadership's Situation–Behavior–Impact model structures feedback around observable behaviour rather than personality, "significantly reducing defensiveness." This aligns with Sero's "neutral observation before challenge" rule and with the "Observation(2)" phase of "Something feels off."

**7. Onboarding research supports friction-removal and role-clarity, not assessment.** Bauer's Four C's (Compliance, Clarification, Culture, Connection; SHRM Foundation, 2010) and her meta-analytic work (Bauer et al., JAP, 2007) show newcomer adjustment — role clarity, self-efficacy, social acceptance — mediates onboarding outcomes (job satisfaction, commitment, performance, retention, lower turnover). Early assessment is not part of the adjustment mechanism. This validates Sero's "friction-removal, not assessment" tone for onboarding.

**8. Wellbeing-conversation guidance is emphatically observation-first, opt-in, non-diagnostic.** NICE guideline NG212 (Mental wellbeing at work), the CIPD, and WHO guidelines converge: managers should notice changes, open a door, lead with care not performance, avoid making assumptions or judgements, "be led by the employee," and never diagnose. This strongly validates Sero's "Something feels off" arc design and its No-Inference Standard.

## Details: Per-Arc Verdict Tables

### Arc 1 — Bi-weekly check-in (6 questions): Pulse(1) → Friction(2) → Momentum(2) → Lift(1)
Tone: casual, peer-tempered.

| Phase | Assessment | Evidence alignment |
|---|---|---|
| Pulse(1) | **Keep** | Matches Rogelberg's "human opening" and Gallup's "care about me as a person" item. One question is right; don't over-invest. |
| Friction(2) | **Keep** | Blocker-removal is a core productivity function of 1:1s (Rogelberg). Solution-focused framing applies. |
| Momentum(2) | **Keep** | Strengths-use and progress questions map to Gallup engagement items and SF coaching. |
| Lift(1) | **Keep** | Forward-looking support question; consistent with employee-led ethos. |

Verdict: **Well-formed.** Six questions in a bi-weekly (likely 30-min) slot is realistic. No phase changes needed.
**Machine-enforceable anti-pattern:** *No performance-coded or evaluative questions* (block rating language, "are you on track", competency terms) — the relational check-in must not become a covert status/appraisal meeting.

### Arc 2 — Performance & feedback (8 questions): Self-read(1) → Evidence(2) → Gap-naming(2) → Cause(2) → Commit(1)
Tone: **direct, no cushioning** ← flagged.

| Phase | Assessment | Evidence alignment |
|---|---|---|
| Self-read(1) | **Keep, reframe** | Supported as defensiveness-reducer/voice (Cawley et al. ρ=0.61; Bassett & Meyer 1968) — but must not be treated as the rating (Mabe & West r=0.29). Keep as opener; ensure manager evidence follows. |
| Evidence(2) | **Keep** | SBI: observable situation/behaviour before impact. Strong fit. |
| Gap-naming(2) | **Keep** | Task-directed gap naming is the productive core (Kluger & DeNisi). Must stay task-level, not person-level. |
| Cause(2) | **Change/merge** | "Cause" invites "why" and root-cause attribution that easily becomes person-directed and speculative. Merge into a single forward-looking question or reframe to "what would change the outcome". |
| Commit(1) | **Keep** | Commitment/goal-setting improves performance (GE studies; GROW "Will"). |

Verdict: **Structurally sound but two problems.** (a) The **"no cushioning" tone label is off-evidence**: relabel to "task-directed, specific, non-personal." (b) **Cause(2) is the weakest phase** — collapse to 1 question, dropping the arc to 7. The Self-read opener is fine but should be explicitly framed as "your read," not the verdict.
**Machine-enforceable anti-pattern:** *No person-directed / trait language in feedback questions* (block "you are [trait]", character/ability attributions; require task/behaviour referents) — this operationalises Kluger & DeNisi's task-vs-person distinction as a gate.

### Arc 3 — Growth & career (9 questions): Anchor(2) → Aspiration(2) → Gap(2) → Investment(2) → Commitment(1)
Tone: coach, not HR.

| Phase | Assessment | Evidence alignment |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor(2) | **Keep, consider merge to 1** | Values/strengths anchoring is sound but 2 questions plus 8 others is long. |
| Aspiration(2) | **Keep** | GROW "Goal." Stay-interview evidence: career-growth dissatisfaction is a major attrition driver. |
| Gap(2) | **Keep** | GROW "Reality." |
| Investment(2) | **Keep** | GROW "Options." |
| Commitment(1) | **Keep** | GROW "Will." |

Verdict: **Best-aligned arc (tracks GROW, pooled effect size 0.43).** Only issue is **length: 9 questions needs a 45–60 min slot.** Merge Anchor to 1 question → 8 total, or gate to a longer meeting.
**Machine-enforceable anti-pattern:** *No promotion/compensation promises* (block commitment-phase outputs containing promise language re: title, pay, timeline) — keeps it "coach, not HR" and avoids psychological-contract breach.

### Arc 4 — Something feels off (6 questions): Landing(1) → Observation(2) → Underneath(2) → Support(1)
Tone: observation-first, opt-in.

| Phase | Assessment | Evidence alignment |
|---|---|---|
| Landing(1) | **Keep** | "No agenda, just checking in" opener — matches NICE/CIPD "open a door, lead with care." |
| Observation(2) | **Keep** | SBI observation-first; NICE "don't make assumptions." Must be declared observable facts only (fits No-Inference Standard). |
| Underneath(2) | **Change/rename** | "Underneath" risks diagnosis/probing psychological states. Rename to "opt-in exploration," strictly employee-led. Keep 2 Q but gate language. |
| Support(1) | **Keep** | "How can I best support you?" — matches wellbeing guidance giving employee agency. |

Verdict: **Design matches best-practice wellbeing guidance closely.** Main risk is the runner allowing diagnostic/inferential phrasing in "Underneath."
**Machine-enforceable anti-pattern:** *No diagnosis / state-inference language* (block "burnout", "depressed", "disengaged", "stressed", clinical/state terms in manager questions) — directly enforces the No-Inference Standard and NICE "don't diagnose."

### Arc 5 — Onboarding (6 questions): Settling(1) → Orientation(2) → Blockers(2) → Connection(1)
Tone: friction-removal, not assessment.

| Phase | Assessment | Evidence alignment |
|---|---|---|
| Settling(1) | **Keep** | Newcomer adjustment/comfort (Bauer). |
| Orientation(2) | **Keep** | Role clarity — the strongest mediator of onboarding outcomes (Bauer). |
| Blockers(2) | **Keep** | Friction removal; self-efficacy. |
| Connection(1) | **Keep** | Bauer's "Connection" C — social acceptance predicts retention. |

Verdict: **Well-aligned with Bauer's Four C's.** Note the arc omits "Compliance," which is appropriate (that's an HR/admin function, not a 1:1 conversation). **Onboarding check-ins should stay friction-removal only; no assessment elements** — this is correct per the evidence, since early evaluation is not part of the adjustment mechanism and would undercut psychological safety (Edmondson, ASQ, 1999).
**Machine-enforceable anti-pattern:** *No evaluative/assessment questions* (block "are you meeting expectations", performance-rating and judgement terms) during the onboarding window.

## Ranked list of concrete changes (strongest evidence first)

1. **Relabel the Performance arc tone from "direct, no cushioning" to "direct, task-directed, non-personal," and add a gate blocking person/trait-coded feedback questions.** *Evidence: Kluger & DeNisi 1996, weighted mean d = 0.41 but >38% of feedback interventions reduced performance; effectiveness falls as attention moves "closer to the self and away from the task."* Strongest and most quantitatively robust finding in scope.

2. **Add a deterministic "no state-inference / no diagnosis" gate to "Something feels off" (and enforce across relational arcs).** *Evidence: NICE NG212, CIPD, WHO — be led by the employee, don't assume or diagnose.* Also enforces Sero's No-Inference Standard mechanically.

3. **Keep "Self-read(1)" as the Performance opener but frame it as voice, not rating.** *Evidence: Cawley, Keeping & Levy 1998, participation–reactions ρ = 0.61 (value-expressive voice strongest); Bassett & Meyer 1968 (reduced defensiveness); counter-weight Mabe & West 1982, self-rating validity r = 0.29.*

4. **Trim Performance "Cause(2)" to 1 question (arc 8→7) to prevent "why"-driven, person-directed root-causing.** *Evidence: solution-focused > problem-focused questions, Grant & Gerrard 2019 (~11% negative-affect reduction); Kluger & DeNisi task-focus.*

5. **Gate the 8- and 9-question arcs (Performance, Growth) to a 45–60 min meeting length; keep 6-question arcs at 30 min.** *Evidence: 1:1 duration norms 30–60 min; 70–80% direct-report talk-time (Rogelberg/HBR) limits manager questions to ~4–6 in 30 min.*

6. **Merge Growth "Anchor(2)" to 1 question (arc 9→8).** *Evidence: GROW model pooled effect size 0.43; length/pacing constraint.*

7. **Add a "no performance-coded questions" gate to the bi-weekly check-in.** *Evidence: Rogelberg — relational/development functions must not be crowded out by status; Edmondson psychological safety.*

8. **Add a "no promotion/pay promise" gate to Growth "Commitment."** *Evidence: stay-interview/career research; avoids psychological-contract breach; keeps "coach, not HR."*

9. **Rename "Something feels off → Underneath" to an opt-in, employee-led exploration phase.** *Evidence: wellbeing guidance opt-in principle; SBI observation-before-interpretation.*

10. **Keep onboarding assessment-free.** *Evidence: Bauer Four C's — adjustment (clarity, self-efficacy, acceptance) mediates outcomes; assessment is not a mechanism.*

## On the repeated 1-2-2-1 shape (Question 4)
The shared arithmetic shape is **a defensible template, not a sign of undifferentiated meeting types.** What the evidence says needs to differ between conversation types is *content, sequence logic, and tone* — not the number of questions per phase. The evaluative arc (Performance) is built on a feedback-intervention spine (self-view → evidence → task-gap → commitment); the coaching arc (Growth) is built on GROW; the wellbeing arc is built on observation-first opt-in guidance; the onboarding arc is built on Bauer's adjustment mechanism. These are genuinely different structures that happen to compress to similar phase counts because human working memory and a 30-minute slot impose similar constraints on all of them. The real differentiation risk is *tone-bleed* — a relational meeting acquiring evaluative content — which is why the anti-pattern gates (not the phase counts) are where differentiation must be enforced.

## Recommendations
- **Stage 1 (ship now):** Implement gates #1, #2, #8, #10 — these are mechanically checkable keyword/pattern gates and carry the strongest evidence. Relabel the Performance tone.
- **Stage 2:** Implement the length-gating (#5) and the two question-count trims (#4, #6). Benchmark to change: if telemetry shows Performance/Growth 1:1s routinely run <35 min, trim harder; if they run 50–60 min, the current 8–9 counts are fine.
- **Stage 3:** Rename/reframe phases (#3, #9) and add the check-in gate (#7).
- **Threshold that would change the plan:** If Sero later gains reliable meeting-length and talk-time telemetry, revisit question budgets empirically rather than by heuristic. If user testing shows managers routinely skipping the Self-read opener, consider making it optional rather than fixed.

## Caveats (fact / assumption / unknown)
- **Fact:** The effect sizes cited (Kluger & DeNisi d=0.41, >38% negative; Cawley et al. ρ=0.61; Mabe & West r=0.29; GROW pooled 0.43; Gallup 70% variance / 3x engagement; GE-study defensiveness findings) are from peer-reviewed or primary sources as stated.
- **Assumption:** That Sero's bi-weekly check-in, "Something feels off," and onboarding arcs run in ~30-min slots and the Performance/Growth arcs can be given 45–60 min. Question-count realism depends on this.
- **Unknown / thin evidence:** There is **no modern RCT** directly testing conversation *order* (self-assess-first vs manager-rating-first) in real manager–employee reviews; the supporting evidence (Bassett & Meyer 1968) is 55+ years old and confounded with other design changes. Participation benefits are also **moderated by employee personality** (Wexley, Singh & Yukl, JAP, 1973), so "self-read first" is not universally optimal. Motivational-interviewing transfer (sustain-talk predicting worse outcomes; Magill et al. 2014) is analogical, from clinical not workplace settings. The GROW meta-analysis is drawn from educator, not corporate-manager, samples.
